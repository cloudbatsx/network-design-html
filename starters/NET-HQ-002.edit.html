Section 0

Overview — what this document is

  • Dual-homed Internet edge. Two carriers (ISP-A, ISP-B) terminate on an independent router each; eBGP from AS 65010 with default-only acceptance. No single carrier or router failure isolates the site.
  • Active/standby NGFW pair. SJC-FW-01/02 in high-availability; all north-south traffic is inspected. Failover is stateful over a dedicated 2×10G bundle.
  • Collapsed core/distribution. Two chassis run as one logical switch (StackWise Virtual). Every downstream device dual-homes into it with a port-channel — one member per physical chassis — so a chassis loss is a link-member loss, not an outage.
  • Three IDF access switches plus a server access switch, each on a 2×10G multi-chassis EtherChannel (MEC) uplink. 12 access points on CAPWAP to a single controller.
  • Out-of-band management is physically separate — its own switch, its own VLAN, no dependency on the production data path.
  • Open finding: rack power does not survive a single-feed loss at the designed load. Quantified in §9; remediation options given. This is a deliberate demonstration of the checklist catching a real defect.

Routing design

ProtocolWherePolicy
eBGPWAN-01 → ISP-A
WAN-02 → ISP-B		 AS 65010. Inbound: accept default route only — a full table is not needed and a prefix-limit of 10 protects against a leak. Outbound: advertise the site NAT block only, with an explicit prefix-list. No transit.
iBGPWAN-01 ⇄ WAN-02 Single peering over link 3 so either router can reach the other carrier’s default if its own eBGP session drops.
StaticFW pair → WAN Default route via the directly attached router; the standby FW inherits it on failover.
OSPFv2FW pair ⇄ SJC-CORE Area 0 across Po10/Po11. Core originates a default into the campus; FW redistributes connected transit only.
— none —Campus gateways No FHRP. StackWise Virtual presents one control plane, so each VLAN has a single SVI owned by the logical switch. HSRP/VRRP would be redundant here — a common over-specification worth stating explicitly so a reviewer does not read its absence as an omission.

Finding R1 — the rack does not survive a single-feed loss At the designed load of 5300 W, either feed alone sits at 106 % of its derated capacity. The breaker trips and the rack goes dark — the dual-feed topology buys nothing. Three ways out, in order of preference:
  1. Upgrade both circuits to 208 V / 40 A. Usable becomes 6656 VA; worst case falls to 79.6 %. Cleanest fix, keeps the rack intact.
  2. Relocate SJC-ESX-01/02 (1240 W) to another rack. Worst case falls to 81.3 % on the existing 30 A feeds. No electrical work, but it splits the compute away from its access switch.
  3. Accept with a documented load-shed policy that drops the ESX hosts on single-feed operation. Cheapest, and the least honest — it only works if someone actually implements the shed.
Recommendation: option 1. Resolve before the reserved U13–16 growth is populated, which would add load on top of this.

Section 6

Open items, assumptions & risk register

IDItemImpactSeverityAction
R1Single-feed power overload. Either feed alone carries 106 % of derated capacity (§9). Breaker trips on a feed loss; the whole rack goes dark. Dual-feed topology delivers no benefit. HIGH Upgrade both circuits to 208 V / 40 A before energising. Options in §9.
R2Reversed airflow on the core pair. SJC-CORE-01/02 shipped with port-side-exhaust fan trays — air moves rear → front. Exhausts hot-aisle air into the cold aisle and ingests its own exhaust. Thermal trip risk for the two most critical devices in the site. HIGH Swap to port-side-intake trays before the aisle is contained. Marker ① on Figure 2.
R3Two single-corded devices. SJC-OOB-01 and SJC-CON-01 have one PSU, both fed from A. An A-feed failure takes out out-of-band management — precisely when it is needed most. MEDIUM Fit a 1U rack ATS on the OOB pair, or move one to feed B and accept split risk. Marker ② on Figure 2.
R4Weights and power are datasheet typical, not measured. Real draw varies with optics count and PoE load; the §9 margins could shift. MEDIUM Meter each feed at commissioning and re-issue §9 with measured values.
R5FPR-4215 corrected to 1RU. The 4200-series datasheet lists the 4215 at 1RU; through rev 2.1 the elevation modelled both firewalls at 3U and the schedule matched the drawing rather than the hardware. Resolved. Both firewalls are scheduled at the datasheet height; U23–24 and U27–28 are reclaimed and blanked. LOW Verify against the physical rack on the next site visit. Heights elsewhere in §8 remain datasheet values, not purchase orders.
R6Guest wireless egresses via SJC-FW-01 only (VLAN 41, note ③). Guest SSID has no path on an FW-01 failure. Corporate wireless is unaffected. LOW Accepted — guest access is non-critical. Documented rather than silently designed in.
R7U15–16 reserved for a 100G spine pair that does not exist yet. Populating it adds load on top of an already-failing R1. MEDIUM R1 must be closed first. Sequence the two changes.
