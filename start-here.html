  1. Open edit-with-ai.html — it sits right at the top of what you downloaded.
  2. Press Start a new design. The blank template is built in — no file to find. The progress rail along the top now walks you through everything that follows.

  4. Press Copy the build prompt — type nothing — and paste it into that same chat.
  5. Copy the AI's reply → paste it into step 3 → Check it. Problems? Copy problems → paste to the AI → paste its new reply → check again.

  9. Want official Cisco artwork in it? The Package step appears right under Save: one click on the assets folder, one click to build. Anything the folder lacks keeps its drawn artwork — a partial package is a valid package.

Everything below is this list, slowed down and illustrated. Read it when you want depth — not before you start. From your second document on, Open one of my designs replaces step 2, and your branding carries forward.

What you are going to end up with

So you know what you are aiming at before you start.

One file. It opens in any browser, it prints to a clean PDF, and it holds four things that normally live in four different tools:

The one idea to hold on to

You never edit the drawing. You never touch HTML. You describe a change in a sentence, a free AI writes a small block of data, and the file draws itself from that data. Everything else in the file is copied across untouched, character for character.

0

Before you open anything, write down what you know

Ten minutes here saves an hour of arguing with an AI later.

You are documenting a network you already have (or one you are proposing). Get it out of your head and onto a scrap of paper first. You need four short lists:

DevicesEvery box you want on the picture. A short id and what it is: firewall, router, core switch, access point, server.
ConnectionsWhat plugs into what. You do not need port numbers — just "this one connects to that one".
AreasThe groupings a reader needs: outside, perimeter/DMZ, internal, management.
Rack contentsOnly if you are documenting a physical rack: what is mounted, how tall it is in U, and where.
Were you handed a diagram? Then the lists already exist — extract them

Attach the picture to your AI chat and say: “Describe this network in detail: every device with a suggested short id, every connection, and the trust areas — as three plain-text lists.” Then read the lists and correct what it misread. You know your organisation; the AI only knows pixels. Those corrected lists are this whole step, done — and keep that chat open, because every prompt that follows can use them. (That prompt lives behind the helper's Copy prompt 1 — read my picture button — you never retype it.)

Pick device ids now, and keep them boring

An id is the short name the document uses everywhere. core-sw-01. edge-fw-02. sea-acc-01. Lower case, no spaces, numbered. This matters more than it looks:

The same device uses the same id in the drawing and in the rack

That shared id is the thread tying the two views together. If the picture calls it core-sw-01 and the rack calls it coresw1, the document thinks they are two different machines — and so will the next person to read it.

Write down what you are not sure about

Seriously. "Nobody has tested the firewall failover." "I think that link is 1 Gbps but I have not checked." These go in the document as gaps, and they are the most valuable part of it. A drawing that looks confident about something nobody verified is worse than no drawing at all.

5

Change one part at a time

This is the single most important habit. It is why a free model can do this.

Your whole design might be 39,000 characters of data. A free AI has a limit on how much it can write in one reply — ask for everything and it gets cut off half way, which is the most common way this goes wrong.

So the dropdown in step 2 lets you ask for one part. The AI only rewrites that part; the helper puts it back into your untouched file.

A second, quieter benefit

An AI asked only for the rack cannot reword your findings or move devices on the drawing. Narrowing the request narrows the blast radius.

So plan your work as a short list of small edits

Came through Door A? The one “Everything” run already produced your first draft — this list is the order to refine it in, one part at a time:

  1. Devices — rename the example devices to yours, add and remove until the list is right.
  2. Connections — wire them up.
  3. Areas — adjust the zone boxes if your grouping differs.
  4. The rack — if you have one.
  5. Cover details — your title, drawing number, author, date. Your logo and organisation name have their own Branding panel in the helper — no AI involved.
  6. Gaps — everything you are not sure about.
  7. Notes & tables — the written record, last.
6

How to phrase the request

Plain English. Be specific about names, vague about everything else.

Good — “Rename edge-fw-01 to mel-fw-01, and add a second firewall mel-fw-02 next to it in the perimeter area.”
Good — “Remove the three example access switches. Add two: mel-acc-01 and mel-acc-02, both in the internal area.”
Weak — “Make it match our network.” The AI has no idea what your network is. It will invent one, confidently.

You do not need to mention coordinates, icon names, colours or file formats. The prompt the helper builds already contains the rules, the list of valid device types, your current data, and the ids the rest of the document depends on.

Choosing what a device looks like

If you name the kind of thing it is, the AI will pick from the twenty available device types. They are:

cloud · router · firewall · core-switch · access-switch · access-point
server · pc · workstation · wlan-controller · vpn-headend · dc-switch
ip-phone · laptop · printer · database · storage-array
network-management · branch-office · wireless-router

Not on the list? Say the closest one — “treat the load balancer as a server”. Made-up types draw a red error box, and the checker will catch it before you save.

7

Press Check it and read what it says

This is the step that makes the whole thing safe for a beginner.

Paste the AI's whole reply — code fences, a stray “Sure, here you go!”, a trailing comma, it cleans all of that up. Then one of three things happens.

What you seeWhat it meansWhat to do
No problems found The data is complete and consistent. Read the change list, then save.
Ready to save, with N warnings It will draw, but something is off — devices overlapping, a device half in and half out of an area, an empty gaps list. Read them. Fix if they matter; save if they do not.
N problems must be fixed Saving would produce a broken document. Press Copy problems, paste it back to the AI, paste its new reply, check again.

The messages are written for humans, not for engineers:

access-sw-01 and edge-fw-02 both occupy U38.
Free units are: 1, 2, 3, 12, 13, 30, 31.

Connection 4 points at "acc-sw-03", which is not a device on
this diagram. The connection will not be drawn.

"wlc-01" is drawn off the right edge of the page.

Gap 2 is pinned to "core-sw-09", which is not a device on this
diagram. The marker will not be drawn.
You are never stuck in a loop

Copy problems puts a ready-made message on your clipboard — the problem and the instruction to fix it. Paste it straight into the same AI chat. Two rounds is normal. If a third round fails, make your request smaller instead of louder.

8

Read the change list before you save

Passing the checks proves the file is not broken. It does not prove the AI only did what you asked.

An AI told to add one switch can also reword a caption, renumber an address, or quietly drop a device from your rack — and every check above would still pass, because the result is perfectly valid data. So step 4 shows you a plain list of every single difference:

If you see something you did not ask for, do not save. Go back to the AI and say “you also renamed core-sw-01 — leave it alone and send that part again.”

If it says “Nothing changed”

The AI sent back exactly what you already had. Either it agreed with you, or — far more likely — it did not understand the request. Rephrase and try again.

9

Save, and open the result

Then go round again for the next part.

Press Save new design file. Your browser downloads a new .edit.html. Double-click it and look at the drawing.

Everything outside the data block is copied across byte for byte — the drawing code, the symbols, the styling are all identical to what you started with. The helper refuses to save at all if that is not true, so you cannot end up with a mangled file.

Keep the new file — it is your undo history

Each save produces a new file. If you keep the helper tab open, it carries on from the file it just saved, so your next edit builds on this one automatically. Coming back later? Load your newest saved file in step 1 — not the original starter. Keep the intermediate copies until you are happy.

10

Two things about position that confuse everyone

You rarely need these — but when a drawing looks wrong, it is almost always one of them.

On the drawing: a device's position is its centre

In the rack: units are counted from the bottom

Faceplate heights are checked for you too

A drawn faceplate's name ends in its height — generic-server-2u is two units tall. Put it on a 1U device and the checker stops you before saving, naming both numbers, so a mismatched face never reaches the document.

11

Say what you do not know

The part everyone skips, and the part that makes the document worth reading.

Every design has holes: things nobody tested, numbers nobody confirmed, decisions made years ago for reasons nobody wrote down. This document has a place for exactly that, and it will nag you if you leave it empty.

Pick Gaps & open items in the dropdown and say something like:

“Add three gaps: firewall failover has never been tested; the link to the second core is assumed to be 10 Gbps but unverified; there is no out-of-band management path. Pin the first one to mel-fw-01 and the last one to the internal area.”

“Pinning” puts a numbered marker on the drawing, on the exact device the gap is about — so the picture and the list can never disagree. Roughly half your gaps pinned is a good document; pinning everything makes the drawing unreadable.

Why this beats a confident-looking diagram

A reader can act on “we think this is 10 Gbps, nobody has checked”. They cannot act on a clean line with a label. The eye control lets you present without them and print without them — but the document cannot be saved with an empty gaps list, so the honesty is in the file whether or not a given copy shows it.

Optional: swap in official Cisco artwork

Only when a document needs it. Your file is already finished without this.

Your design draws with the project's own vector symbols. They are clean, they print beautifully, and the whole file is about 200 KB. For a customer deliverable or a house standard, you may want the official Cisco icons and photographic rack faces instead.

  1. The easy way: right after you save in the helper, the Package step appears — your just-saved design is already loaded. Choose the assets folder once, press build, done. Anything the folder lacks keeps its built-in drawn artwork, and the report names it — a partial package is a valid package.
  2. The standalone way — for a file someone sent you: open packager.html (at the top of the repository, beside the helper), press Choose artwork folder → the assets folder, then Choose design file, then Build portable HTML.

Got your own artwork — a company device photo, a vendor's icon? Drop the image files in with Add my own images and refer to them by exact filename. Anything the design asks for that you have not supplied is listed by name before the build runs, so nothing fails silently.

When something breaks

Every one of these has a boring cause and a two-minute fix.

What you seeWhat it means, and what to do
The AI's reply is rejectedUsually it added an explanation around the JSON, or wrapped it in code fences. Say: “Reply with only the JSON object, nothing before or after.”
The AI ran out of room mid-replyYou asked for too much at once. Pick a narrower part — that is exactly what the dropdown is for.
A red error box in the diagramAn icon name that does not exist. There are exactly 20 valid ones; switch, fw and nas are not among them. Tell the AI to use the closest name from the allowed list.
A connection is invisibleAn unrecognised link kind. The valid ones are l3, aggregate, ha, access and backup.
A black rectangle over the diagramAn unrecognised area kind. Use external, perimeter, internal or other.
Odd characters like Â· everywhereThe file got saved in the wrong encoding. Always save as UTF-8, never open these files in Word, and go back to your last good save.
The helper refuses the file as a retired sheet editionYou loaded a copy downloaded before the four engineering sheets were regenerated through the engine. Re-download the current starter — every current kit is fully AI-editable, drawings included.
Everything looks broken after an editClose it. Open your previous saved file — every save made a new file, so the old one is still there.
Four rules that prevent almost all of the above

Do not edit the HTML by hand. Do not change anything outside the two PROOF-DATA marker lines. Do not open these files in Word, ever. And do not send anyone a document whose badge says FAIL.

Words this project uses

TopologyThe logical diagram — what exists and what connects to what. Not where it physically sits.
Rack elevationThe physical view — a front and rear picture of the cabinet, drawn from one equipment list so the two faces can never disagree.
U / rack unit1.75 inches of cabinet height. Devices are 1U, 2U, 3U and so on, and they are counted from the bottom.
Zone / areaA labelled box grouping devices that share a trust level or a role — Internet, Perimeter, Office LAN.
HA pairTwo devices acting as one, so either can fail. Drawn with the ha link kind.
Node / device idThe short name — core-sw-01 — shared by the diagram, the rack, the tables and the gaps list.
The data blockThe JSON between the PROOF-DATA markers. The only part that ever changes. The AI sees this and nothing else.
Editable vs portableEditable is the light file you work in. Portable is the packaged one with official images inside, and it can hand the editable version back at any time.

A last word on what this is

This is documentation tooling, not discovery. It will not poll your network, monitor it, or replace a source of truth like NetBox. What it does is make intentional design documentation easy to write, easy to review, and honest about its own gaps — which is normally the hardest kind to produce.
