Section 0

Overview — what this document is

Purpose, declared scope, topology inventory, and the release posture of this current standardized edition.

Design intent

  • Preserve the source topology from Internet and MPLS services through edge, perimeter, core, DMZ, campus, application, and OOB networks.
  • Apply the common Seattle, San Jose, private-cloud fabric, and enterprise-lab document system without inventing physical implementation facts.
  • Keep every redundant routed relationship individually traceable and every shared network represented as a segment with explicit junctions.
  • Provide complete node, link, segment, addressing, risk, QA, provenance, and revision records beside the figure.

Scope & exclusions

This is a proposed training/reference design, not an as-built record or construction package. Figure 1 is strictly Layer 3 logical. It deliberately omits Layer 2 access switches, STP, trunk allow-lists, physical interface assignments, cable paths, rack positions, power feeds, airflow, serials, and a bill of materials.

Practices carried forward

  • One sheet, one abstraction layer: this is Layer 3 logical, not a physical cabling or rack drawing.
  • Official Cisco conceptual artwork remains unmodified; product models belong in text or inventory.
  • Redundant peers are side-by-side, links are orthogonal, and all line semantics appear in the live legend.
  • Shared networks use subnet bars and junctions instead of many repeated point-to-point lines.
  • Hostnames, loopbacks, routing context, status, revision, and cross-references are explicit.

Deliberate omissions

  • No access switches, STP state, trunk allow-lists, or physical interface labels; those belong on L2 and L1 sheets.
  • No rack units, power feeds, airflow, or front/rear device faces; those belong on a rack elevation.
  • No arrowheads because this sheet describes bidirectional links and adjacencies, not traffic flow.
  • No production claims: the design is fictional and marked proposed/training throughout.

Section 5

Operations & observability — declared out of scope

This document draws no physical plant, so it measures none. That boundary is stated rather than left implicit.

  • No power, thermal or environmental record exists in this document. There is no rack, no PDU, no circuit and no measurement — not because they were overlooked, but because §3 declares physical placement out of scope. Inventing them here would manufacture evidence.
  • No monitoring system is defined. No NMS, flow collector, syslog target or metrics store is named for any object in Figure 1, so nothing in this design is currently observable.
  • Out-of-band management is drawn but unmeasured. The OOB segment appears in the topology as a routed identity only; no console server, no reachability test and no failure-mode evidence is attached to it.

These are the observability facts a companion physical document would have to supply. Until one exists, every operational claim about this design is unverified — see §6.

Logical symbol source

The topology uses the official Cisco conceptual icon vocabulary for clouds, routers, firewalls, a load balancer, servers, multilayer switches, endpoints, and network management.

  • Cisco Network Topology Icons
  • Artwork is embedded into this file; the citation is not a runtime dependency.

Reuse boundary

Conceptual symbols identify roles, not exact models. Do not infer chassis, licensing, interface availability, transceivers, rack placement, or power from Figure 1. Product selection belongs in an approved inventory and physical design.

Topology grammar

  • Internet and WAN north; enterprise services south.
  • A/odd member left; B/even member right.
  • Orthogonal paths; white halo means crossing without connection.
  • Junction circles mean an intentional shared-segment attachment.
  • No arrowheads because links are bidirectional.
